[markdown] <p class='lead'>The papers, data and tools behind this site, and what each one was used for.</p>
[markdown] **[1]** Lux, T. & Marchesi, M. (1999). Scaling and criticality in a stochastic multi-agent model of a financial market. *Nature* 397, 498–500. [doi:10.1038/17290](https://doi.org/10.1038/17290)  
<span class='muted'>The model this site rebuilds, and the results it reproduces.</span>
[markdown] **[2]** Samanidou, E., Zschischang, E., Stauffer, D. & Lux, T. (2007). Agent-based models of financial markets. *Rep. Prog. Phys.* 70, 409–450. [arXiv:physics/0701140](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0701140)  
<span class='muted'>Every equation of the model (docs/SPEC.md).</span>
[markdown] **[3]** Cristelli, M., Pietronero, L. & Zaccaria, A. (2011). Critical overview of agent-based models for economics. [arXiv:1101.1847](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.1847)  
<span class='muted'>The same equations, checked a second time.</span>
[markdown] **[4]** Lux, T. & Marchesi, M. (2000). Volatility clustering in financial markets: a microsimulation of interacting agents. *Int. J. Theor. Appl. Finance* 3, 675–702.  
<span class='muted'>The paper's settings tables (not yet obtained, so this site fits its own).</span>
[markdown] **[5]** Kirman, A. (1993). Ants, rationality, and recruitment. *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 108, 137–156.  
<span class='muted'>The ant model behind herding, checked against its exact formula first.</span>
[markdown] **[6]** Dewachter, H. & Lyrio, M. The economic value of technical trading rules: a nonparametric utility-based approach.  
<span class='muted'>Why trend chasers (chartists) are worth modelling: traders really do trade on past price patterns.</span>
[markdown] **[7]** Gopikrishnan, P. et al. (1998). Inverse cubic law for the distribution of stock price variations. *Eur. Phys. J. B* 3, 139–140. [arXiv:cond-mat/9803374](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9803374)  
<span class='muted'>The target for the tail exponent: about 3.</span>
[markdown] **[8]** Cont, R. (2001). Empirical properties of asset returns: stylized facts and statistical issues. *Quantitative Finance* 1, 223–236. [doi:10.1080/713665670](https://doi.org/10.1080/713665670)  
<span class='muted'>The textbook checklist of patterns in real markets.</span>
[markdown] **[9]** Peng, C.-K. et al. (1994). Mosaic organization of DNA nucleotides. *Phys. Rev. E* 49, 1685–1689.  
<span class='muted'>The method that measures how long wild spells last (DFA, Hurst H).</span>
[markdown] **[10]** Bollerslev, T. (1986). Generalized autoregressive conditional heteroskedasticity. *J. Econometrics* 31, 307–327. [doi:10.1016/0304-4076(86)90063-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-4076(86)90063-1)  
<span class='muted'>GARCH: a rival formula for bursts of wild days, with no traders in it.</span>
[markdown] **[11]** Glosten, L., Jagannathan, R. & Runkle, D. (1993). On the relation between the expected value and the volatility of the nominal excess return on stocks. *J. Finance* 48, 1779–1801.  
<span class='muted'>The part of GARCH that makes falls bigger than rises.</span>
[markdown] **[12]** Bouchaud, J.-P., Matacz, A. & Potters, M. (2001). Leverage effect in financial markets: the retarded volatility model. *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 87, 228701. [doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.87.228701](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.87.228701)  
<span class='muted'>The "panic" idea, tested here. It does not make falls bigger than rises.</span>
[markdown] **[13]** Yahoo Finance.  
<span class='muted'>The S&P 500 (^GSPC), the Nasdaq-100, 5 stocks, gold and copper, and live prices.</span>
[markdown] **[14]** FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis.  
<span class='muted'>A second copy of the S&P 500 to check against, and Brent oil.</span>
[markdown] **[15]** MeasuringWorth.  
<span class='muted'>The Dow Jones since 1896.</span>
[markdown] Python, NumPy, SciPy, pandas, Numba, Plotly and Streamlit. The code and its tests are public: [github.com/avishj19/can-herding-explain-market-crashes](https://github.com/avishj19/can-herding-explain-market-crashes).
[caption] The project proposal planned to take the S&P 500 from Stooq. Stooq now blocks automated downloads, so the site uses Yahoo Finance and checks it against an independent copy from FRED: they agree on every day both have (2016 to 2026) to within 0.12%.
