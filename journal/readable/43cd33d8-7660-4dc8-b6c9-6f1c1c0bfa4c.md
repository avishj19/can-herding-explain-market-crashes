**Terms first:**
- **Method of simulated moments (MSM):** the standard name for what your `calibrate.py` does. You compute some statistics ("moments") from the real data and from simulations, then choose the settings that make the two sets closest.
- **Weighting matrix:** the table of weights that says how much each moment counts in the loss.

**What the papers do:**
- **Winker, Gilli & Jeleskovic (2007)** fit models of the currency market to moments like yours (tail shape, volatility clustering, long memory). They get the weights from a **bootstrap**: they resample the real data many times, measure how much each moment wobbles, and give *noisy* moments *less* weight.

### Question 7 of 9: Fig 6b and Kirman's "either/or" story

**Terms:** in Kirman's ant model, each step one ant is picked. ### Question 9 of 9: H|r| "0.81 ± 0.02" or "± 0.03"?

**Terms:** **H of |returns|** (the Hurst exponent of move sizes) measures how long volatility memory lasts. 0.5 means no memory, and higher means big moves cluster for longer.
