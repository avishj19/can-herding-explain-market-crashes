## What the new analysis found
- **Outside news explains 16% of the S&P's daily moves** (2000–2026). With that part removed, the tail exponent moves by only about 0.1 and the memory measure barely changes.
- **Outside news adds the extreme end of the tail.** Without it, the S&P looks like a typical history of the model on all 8 of these measures. With it, only 6 of 8.
- **Surprise Fed moves are 5× a normal day, but there have only been 9.** Scheduled Fed decisions are ordinary days.
- **The links between stocks and oil, and between stocks and interest rates, flip sign over the decades.** Gold barely moves with stocks, even in crashes.
- **Outside markets explain twice as much in wild markets** (33%) as in calm ones (16%). That's the opposite of what herding alone predicts. It doesn't prove news causes the crashes, though: in a panic the stock market also moves oil and rates.
- **"Trading moments" with error bars (Fig 18):** the S&P falls harder than it rises (skewness −0.95), which the model can't do. I added that as a new limitation. Kurtosis turned out to be very uncertain (5.5 to 53), which is a good reason the project relies on tail exponents instead.
