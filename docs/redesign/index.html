    A STUDENT PROJECT · 02-120 · CMU · FALL 2026

    The news follows a bell curve. Traders copy each other. Is that enough to make a crash?

    This site runs a market of 500 simulated traders, side by side with the real S&P 500, to test one idea: that crashes can come from traders copying each other, even when the news is plain bell-curve noise.

    Value traders Optimists Pessimists simulated day

    The model's 500 traders, day by day, through its biggest crash. Each dot is one trader. Watch optimists pile up, then flip to pessimists all at once: that flip is the crash.

    Why build this

    On 19 October 1987 the S&P 500 fell 20% in one day. If daily moves followed a bell curve, a fall that size would come about once in 1086 years (a 1 followed by 86 zeros). It came within decades.

    The question from the proposal: If the news is plain bell-curve noise, can traders copying each other (herding) alone cause the fat tails, crashes and bursts of wild days that real markets show?

    How the program answers it

    STEP 1 · CHECK THE HERDING RULEKirman's ants Ants copy each other when they choose between two food sources. The simulation must match Kirman's exact formula before the market is built on it.
    STEP 2 · BUILD THE MARKET500 traders Value traders buy below the true value and sell above it. Trend chasers follow the trend and copy the crowd's mood. Each trader switches to whichever kind is making more money.
    STEP 3 · COMPAREAgainst the real S&P 500 Same measurements on both: how rare big days are, whether wild days come in bursts, and how long between crashes.

          THE MODEL

          1. Lux, T. & Marchesi, M. (1999). Scaling and criticality in a stochastic multi-agent model of a financial market. Nature 397, 498–500. doi:10.1038/17290 COREThe model this site rebuilds, and the results it reproduces.
          2. Samanidou, E., Zschischang, E., Stauffer, D. & Lux, T. (2007). Agent-based models of financial markets. Rep. Prog. Phys. 70, 409–450. arXiv:physics/0701140 EQUATIONSEvery equation of the model (docs/SPEC.md).
          3. Cristelli, M., Pietronero, L. & Zaccaria, A. (2011). Critical overview of agent-based models for economics. arXiv:1101.1847 CROSS-CHECKThe same equations, checked a second time.
          4. Lux, T. & Marchesi, M. (2000). Volatility clustering in financial markets: a microsimulation of interacting agents. Int. J. Theor. Appl. Finance 3, 675–702. PARAMETERSThe paper's tables of settings (not yet obtained, so this site fits its own).
          5. Kirman, A. (1993). Ants, rationality, and recruitment. Quarterly Journal of Economics 108, 137–156. HERDINGThe ant model, checked against its exact formula first.

          MEASURING MARKETS

          3. Peng, C.-K. et al. (1994). Mosaic organization of DNA nucleotides. Phys. Rev. E 49, 1685–1689. MEMORYThe method that measures how long wild spells last (DFA, Hurst H).
